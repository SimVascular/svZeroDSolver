The \texttt{ChamberCylinder} 0D block was compared with 3D finite-element solutions of the
same thick-walled tube in svMultiPhysics (quadratic tetrahedra, the same material law),
without any 0D data in the 3D model. The comparisons, from simple to complex:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Passive inflation, circumferential fibers} (isotropic, one fiber family, full
Holzapfel-Ogden law; eighth model). Every compared quantity converges to the 0D block
under mesh refinement: the volume at third to fourth order down to $8\cdot10^{-8}$ at
nr = 16 (full Holzapfel-Ogden law, 149k elements), the radial displacement at about 2.8
down to $6\cdot10^{-6}$, the strains and stresses at 1.3--2 down to $1\cdot10^{-4}$--$7\cdot10^{-4}$.
There is no model difference down to these levels.
\item \textbf{Wall thickness and large deformation} (Section~\ref{sec:wall}). A thin
($R_e/R_i = 1.2$) and a very thick wall (2.5) converge at the same rates, the thick wall
down to $1\cdot10^{-6}$ in the volume at nr = 16; a softer isotropic wall that nearly
doubles its volume does not change the differences.
\item \textbf{Free length} (closed ends, Section~\ref{sec:closed}). With the 0D default
\texttt{fixed\_length = 0} the lids carry the cavity pressure and the length is free. The
3D tube is closed by a meshed end plate (svMultiPhysics' caps carry no pressure) and is
six times as long as the test case, so that the end layer at the plate (within 1\% of the
0D radial displacement from three wall thicknesses on) does not reach the mid-length plane. There every
quantity converges to the 0D block, for the full Holzapfel-Ogden wall at nr = 8 to
$8.6\cdot10^{-5}$ in $\rho$ and $3.0\cdot10^{-5}$ in the axial strain.
\item \textbf{0D reference.} The Richardson extrapolations used as the reference agree with
the best estimate of the converged 0D solution to $10^{-8}$ in the volume (Section~\ref{sec:zero-d});
direct 0D solutions with up to 256 elements are less accurate than the extrapolation (with
ne = 256 the volume difference at nr = 16 would read three times too large).
\item \textbf{Passive inflation, helical fibers} (full cylinder with free ends, a tube
twice as long as the test case, $2L_0$). The uniform 0D state carries a section shear that
a free 3D end cannot, so the end layers of the 3D tube deviate; the middle of the $2L_0$
tube is free of this (Section~\ref{sec:helical}), and there every quantity converges to the
0D block: for the $\pm60^\circ$ helix of the test case at nr = 4 to $1.7\cdot10^{-4}$ in the
volume and $9\cdot10^{-4}$ in $\rho$ (order 2.8). With a large twist the twist rate agrees to
$3\cdot10^{-3}$ (uniform $30^\circ$ helix) and $2\cdot10^{-4}$ (helix from $+60^\circ$ to
$0^\circ$) at nr = 2, converging at about fourth order. The $\pm60^\circ$ helix twists very
little (the torques of its two fiber layers almost cancel), so its relative twist error is
larger and converges late: 15\%, 8\% and 1.6\% at nr = 1, 2, 4.
\item \textbf{Active contraction in a circulation}, coupled through svZeroDSolver. With
circumferential fibers the PV loop, volume, stroke volume and wall state converge with the
mesh (stroke volume at order 3.3 to $4\cdot10^{-5}$ at nr = 8); the pressure and valve flows
level off at the difference between the time integrations of the coupled and the
stand-alone models, which halving the time step reduces (pressure $2.3\cdot10^{-3}$ to
$8.8\cdot10^{-4}$). At nr = 8 the remaining volume difference also sits early in the cycle
and is time integration; in the ejection it keeps falling with the mesh. A very thick wall
($R_e/R_i = 2.5$, ejection fraction 0.37) converges likewise. With helical fibers ($2L_0$
tube) all differences fall from nr = 1 to 2 (pressure $1.2\cdot10^{-2}$, twist rate
$7\cdot10^{-3}$ at nr = 2). The stroke volume ($2.6\cdot10^{-2}$) is that of the whole tube,
end layers included, which the circulation sees; at the same pressure (0D replay) the
mid-length section matches the 0D stroke volume to $9\cdot10^{-4}$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Status of the runs.} The finest levels ran on Yale's Bouchet cluster: full
Holzapfel-Ogden nr = 16, the helical $2L_0$ tube at nr = 4, the free-length tubes at
nr = 4 and 8, active nr = 8 and the helical active $2L_0$ tube at nr = 2. The wall-thickness, large-deformation and thick-walled active
cases ran on the laptop.
